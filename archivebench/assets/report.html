Quality scores, video format and archive retention
What does VMAF mean?
VMAF estimates perceived video quality against the original under the model’s viewing conditions. 93 does not mean “93% of the original,” and 99 does not mean lossless. Mean scores can hide weak moments. Low 5% describes the lower tail of scored frames, not a confidence interval.
These are ArchiveBench starting points, not Netflix certifications. Review motion, texture and dark scenes. Scores do not prove color-detail preservation or editing latitude. Netflix VMAF FAQ
Resolution and chroma choices were saved as named variants during setup. Shared encoder and quality settings are measured across every planned tuning sample. Reduced-resolution output is upscaled to original dimensions for scoring. Chroma normalization does not quantify all lost 4:2:2 detail. MKV exports and proprietary metadata need an editor compatibility check.
Method, coverage and limitations
  • Selected-source video-only projections average rates within each source, multiply by its full duration, and sum distinct sources. The duration control scales that source mix for another duration. Older reports without source durations use sample-duration extrapolation. Incomplete coverage is exploratory only.
  • Encoding time includes decoding, scaling and disk I/O, but excludes quality scoring, hashing, previews and archive verification. Quick runs have one timing; thorough runs use the median of three.
  • The mean and low-5% collection scores are each the minimum across sample measurements. Frame timelines show scored frames only; quick runs may skip frames.
  • Frontiers connect measured settings, not predictions of untested settings between them. A finite or budget-exhausted search can miss better choices.
  • Originals remain intact. Cleanup requires a separate explicit action after visual review.
